The site is the drawing area. Use Sketch floor on the plan to draw the floor outline. Without a sketch, the whole site is floor.

Criteria

Noise sources add to the background at each point. The target at each point is the higher of 65 dB(A) or the local ambient + 10 dB. The maximum is 105 dB(A).

Noise sources

Preset levels are typical values for sizing only. Replace them with the manufacturer's sound pressure at 1 m, or with site measurements. Each source spreads at −6 dB per doubling of distance from 1 m, and loses the rack and wall reductions along the way. Untick Running for plant that is off when the alarm sounds. Fire pumps and smoke exhaust fans usually run during a fire. Each source appears as a 3D model of its equipment type, drawn to its real footprint on the plan. Height is the acoustic source height; it also sets the mounting height of wall and ceiling fans. Rotation turns the equipment (0° faces +X); the arrow on the plan shows which way fans and forklifts face.

X and Y give the rack corner nearest the origin. "From start" is measured from X = 0 on the long walls, from Y = 0 on the end walls, and from point 1 on an internal wall. A full-height wall runs up to the roof. A path through an internal wall loses the wall's reduction, and a path through a closed door loses the door's reduction. An open door loses nothing. Doors in the outside walls don't change the direct sound, but open ones lower the RT60.

Speaker schedule

How the levels are calculated: each speaker's level is its sensitivity plus 10·log(tap), less inverse-square spreading (−20·log r), less an off-axis loss of −6 dB at half the coverage angle (quadratic, capped at −25 dB). The shadow loss is applied wherever the straight path from speaker to ear passes through a rack. Internal walls reduce any path that passes through them below their top edge. Sound going over or around the ends of a wall (diffraction) is not modelled. Speakers are summed on an energy basis. Ambient noise at each point is the background plus every running noise source, and the target there is the higher of 65 dB(A) or ambient + 10. The optional reverberant field uses Sabine room constant R = Sα/(1−α). X runs along the length, Y across the width, and an aim of 0° points along +X. Sensitivities and taps are taken from the Vigilant Warning System Ancillaries: Audio datasheet (Feb 2019). Where the datasheet gives a range, the coverage angle is averaged across 1 to 4 kHz. This is a design-stage SPL screen. It does not replace STI prediction under AS 1670.4 or measurement at commissioning.
